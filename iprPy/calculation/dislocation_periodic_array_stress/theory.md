## Method and Theory

Dislocation glide is a dynamic process that is activated through a combination of temperature and driving shear stress.  This calculation is designed to simulate such a process by taking a previously generated periodic array of dislocations (PAD) configuration, applying a constant shear stress to the boundary region, and allowing the atoms to naturally evolve during molecular dynamics iterations.  Measuring the resulting strain rate of the configuration provides information about the activation energy of glide.

The PAD configuration as generated by the dislocation_periodic_array calculation method is periodic along the two box vectors within the dislocation's slip plane. The third box direction is non-periodic resulting in two free surfaces that are parallel to the slip plane. The parent dislocation_periodic_array calculation identified boundary atoms as those within a set distance from the two free surfaces. This calculation automatically detects the boundary atoms based on their atom type numbers. The shear stress is applied to the system by adding equal external forces to the atoms in the two boundaries based on the stress values, number of atoms and cross-sectional area of the boundaries. Equal but opposite stresses are applied to the two boundary regions. Two components of stress can be applied called sigma_edge and sigma_screw, which are the driving stresses for glide of the corresponding dislocation characters.

Depending on the rigidbounds setting, the atoms in the boundary region will either be rigid bodies (rigidbounds=True) or allowed to evolve independently (rigidbounds=False).  Using rigid boundaries is usually recommended as it prevents the dislocation from interacting with the free surfaces and provides a closer representation of a bulk system.

The thermo output computes the current shear strains by computing the relative displacements of the two boundary regions in the shearing directions. As the system evolves, there will be an elastic strain response prior to glide activation. The strain rate due to glide can be estimated by measuring strain versus time *after* the elastic response period. Multiple temperatures and stresses may need to be tried to find optimum values in which the dislocation glides without causing additional plastic damage.

Finally, the size of the system along the dislocation line direction matters.  The shorter the periodic distance along the line direction the more confined the dislocation glide mechanisms are.  If the periodicity of the line direction is very short then uniform glide will occur without any dislocation kinking.  Long periodicity is needed to allow for kinking.  The linesizemult setting allows for replicating the initial system along the line direction, thus a single initial "thin" PAD can be used for exploring different periodicities along the line direction.
